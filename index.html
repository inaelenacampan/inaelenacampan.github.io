EMBL EBI - Research Trainee

As a scholar of the French Embassy Program, I joined the Pathogen Informatics and Modelling group, where my main project focused on methods for bacterial pangenome construction. I developed a benchmarking pipeline of 9 methods to evaluate pangenome clustering on both simulated and real data. The real dataset focuses on Streptococcus pneumoniae genomes collected from children under five years old in Southampton (UK). The 6 simulated datasets were generated using the Infinitely Many Genes (IMG) model.

Collège de France - Research assistant

During my first year at ENSAE, I interned at Collège de France under the supervision of Samuel Alizon and Olivier Supplisson. I deployed an R-Shiny interface to visualise the spatial epidemiology of genital ulcers attributable to herpes simplex viruses in France. This project required understanding the epidemiological context, mastering advanced statistical methods—including hierarchical Bayesian modelling and generalised additive models. I was also introduced to addressing spatial confounding through the implementation of various spatial models using INLA and inlabru. I also conducted simulations to compare model performance and ensure robustness of the results.
